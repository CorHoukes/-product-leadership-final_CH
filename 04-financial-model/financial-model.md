> **The case in one paragraph:** The strategic bet. Simplify the daily log to improve the way for field teams document work, while automatically eliminating duplicate reporting and administrative work. (reduce from 14 required fields to 4) -> Contribute to the OKR Reduce time spent creating weekly project status reports by 30% The assumptions: Simplifying the daily log from 14 required fields to 4 and removing duplicate data entry will reduce average completion time from 15 minutes to between 5 and 8 minutes per submission. With at least 90% user adoption across 50 field workers, this is expected to generate 7-10 minutes of realized time savings per worker per day, equivalent to 1.0-1.5 FTEs of annual capacity. The expected return: Reducing the daily log from 14 required fields to 4 is expected to save approximately 10 minutes per field worker per day. Across 50 field workers, this equates to roughly 3,000 hours of productive capacity annually, or 1.5 FTEs of equivalent capacity. Based on estimated labor rates, this represents $150,000-$300,000 in annual productivity value. The initiative requires a one-time investment of $50,000 for design, development, testing, deployment, and training, plus an estimated $15,000 annually for adoption monitoring, process governance, user support, and continuous improvement activities to sustain usage and prevent reversion to duplicate reporting practices. After accounting for the one-time investment and first-year retention effort, the expected net first-year benefit is $85,000-$235,000, with ongoing annual value of $135,000-$285,000 thereafter. The expected payback period is approximately 2-4 months following full deployment. The kill criterion: If, within 90 days of deployment to 50 field workers, average measured productivity gains are less than 7 minutes per worker per day or fewer than 90% of field workers actively use the simplified daily log process, all new development stops and the remaining budget and engineering capacity are reassigned to higher-priority initiatives. No additional funding will be approved without a new business case.

## 2. Kill criteria

> **Kill criterion:** If, within 90 days of deployment to 50 field workers, measured productivity gains average less than 7 minutes per worker per day (equivalent to less than 1 FTE of capacity released annually), all new development stops and the remaining budget and engineering capacity are reassigned to higher-priority initiatives.
