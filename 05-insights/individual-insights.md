# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Lead the Team That Builds with AI

## Friction points

> Initially i thought the hardest part was not being concise enough, but actually it was making deliberate choices about what mattered most. 
> The course repeatedly challenged me to move from ideas/assumptions and feature requests to measurable outcomes and clear success criteria (including Hard No)
> Defining OKRs, building a value-based business case, and establishing kill criteria exposed gaps in my thinking and decision-making.

## Key learnings

> The most valuable learning is the discipline of linking initiatives to strategy and measurable outcomes, 
> One additional key discipline, which i will have to 'force" myself in including is the validated (internal) customer value. 
> (Are we really solving for the (internal) customers?)
> 
> I will continue to use the frameworks to test assumptions, define expected outcomes before development starts, and evaluate whether investments are delivering the intended business impact. On top of this introduce the Kill Criterion
> This will ensure the team, stakeholders as well as leadership are aware and onboard when a new initiative is stopped due to lack of value delivery.
> 
> LLMs can accelerate analysis and challenge thinking, but they are most valuable when combined with sound product judgment and customer insight. (Which was experienced a couple of times during the course.)

## "Aha!" moment

> The initial reason for the chosen transformations to succeed or fail was not based on the right determinant,
> The real determinant is whether my product becomes the easiest channel, not the required channel.
> 
> My biggest realization was the succes of a deployment (including adoption) is not sustained through mandates alone. 
> While requirements can drive initial usage, long-term success depends on whether the product delivers enough value that users naturally choose it over alternatives. 
> Ease of use is a critical component, but the broader determinant is whether the product becomes the most effective and valuable way for users to accomplish their goals.
> (In fact i actually updated my actual product strategy accordingly ;-) )
