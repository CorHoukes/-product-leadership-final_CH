Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Where we play: Large U.S. construction organizations with project portfolios exceeding $75M, focused on superintendents, foremen, and site managers.

How we win: Combining 10+ years of project controls expertise with structured data management and automated dashboarding to reduce reporting effort and improve project visibility.

Positioning: Meridian enables field teams to capture, communicate, and demonstrate progress faster using the tools they already use every day

The hard no

Meridian will not replace enterprise project management tools, but will integrate with them

Objective and key results

Objective: Make Meridian the preferred platform for construction projects

Key Results (KRs)
- Increase active field users by 50% in the next quarter
- Reduce time spent creating weekly project status reports by 30%
- Increase automatically captured project updates by 25% in the next quarter

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Now:
We bet reducing documentation effort, providing proactive updates, and capturing labor activity within existing workflows will increase field adoption, improve coordination, enhance project visibility, and deliver measurable value against established baselines.

Simplify Daily Log --> 14 → 4 fields --> More adoption --> Less admin work --> 30% less reporting effort --> $150K-$300K value

Next:
Enable field teams to document work through photos, voice, and text, capture issues and progress at the source, automatically transform activity into structured updates and project health insights, and demonstrate how adoption drives business outcomes and renewals.

Capture work at source --> More structured updates --> 25% more automated updates --> Better project visibility

Later:
Create AI-powered project documentation, serve as the central communication layer and portfolio source of truth, predict project risks and recommended actions, and proactively identify adoption risks to maximize value realization.

AI Documentation and Risk Prediction --> Proactive project management --> Portfolio source of truth --> Long-term competitive advantage

Trade-off memo

To maximize customer value, adoption, and retention, we recommend prioritizing initiatives that reduce field friction, increase engagement, and address known customer pain points before investing in cosmetic enhancements or niche functionality.

1) Simplify the daily log:
Reduce from 14 required fields to 4 -> Reduce time spent creating weekly project status reports by 30%
2) Notifications:
Introduce proactive push notifications for schedule changes to improve stakeholder coordination and increase project communication captured from 25% to 40%.

3) Time-tracking module:
Embed labor tracking directly into existing field workflows to increase platform engagement and drive a projected 50% increase in active field users. -

Deferred Investments:
- Refresh web UI: Low urgency unless data shows it is affecting conversion or adoption.
- Automated Compliance Checklist Generator: This initiative requires significant development effort while addressing a relatively narrow customer need.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

What this team owns;
The Foundations team owns the capabilities that make Meridian scalable, reusable, and strategically differentiated across all user segments. Specifically, we own:
- Core capture services that turn photos, voice, and text into structured, reusable work records.
- Shared platform capabilities used by both field and enterprise experiences.
- Data model, workflow orchestration, integrations, identity, permissions, and reporting infrastructure.
- AI and automation capabilities that eliminate duplicate reporting and administrative work.
- Technical architecture and platform investments that improve velocity, reliability, security, and scale.

Out of scope;
The Foundations team does not own:
- Field user adoption strategy, rollout plans, training, or change management (PM1).
- Mobile user experience, field workflows, and frontline feature prioritization (PM1).
- Enterprise-specific sales commitments, customer customizations, contract negotiations, or renewal management (PM2).

Joint calls across boundaries;
The following decisions require alignment between Foundations, PM1, and PM2 before commitment or engineering work begins:
- Any feature request requiring more than two sprints of shared engineering capacity.
- Any enterprise commitment that displaces a committed field adoption initiative.
- Any roadmap change affecting a formally approved quarterly objective or key result.
- Any architectural decision that materially changes cost, scalability, security, or future product flexibility.
- Any customer commitment that creates ongoing maintenance obligations for shared platform components.
- Any prioritization decision impacting more than one product team's published roadmap.

No team may unilaterally commit shared engineering capacity for these decisions.

How we decide

Protecting Committed Roadmap Outcomes:
No team may redirect shared engineering capacity from another team's committed roadmap initiative without agreement from the affected PMs or a Product Leadership decision through the defined escalation process.

Cross-team conflicts; When priorities conflict, work will be evaluated against the following criteria: - Criterion - Measure - Weight

1) Strategic Alignment: Expected contribution to quarterly OKRs (0-5) --> 30%
2) User Adoption Impact: Estimated increase in active field users, usage frequency, or task completion (0-5) --> 25%
3) Revenue Impact: Incremental revenue protected or created within 12 months (0-5) --> 20%
4) Efficiency Impact: Hours of administrative work eliminated annually (0-5) --> 15%
5) Delivery Effort & Risk: Relative effort and delivery risk (0-5, inverse scoring) --> 10%

Escalation is required when:
- An initiative causes a delay of more than one sprint to another committed roadmap item.
- More than 20% of a team's planned capacity must be reassigned.
- A conflict impacts a committed quarterly OKR.

Level 1: PM Alignment
Level 2: Product Leadership Review Who: Product Lead / Group Product Manager responsible for the portfolio
Level 3: Executive Portfolio Decision Who: Executive sponsor or product steering group

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

The strategic bet.
Simplify the daily log to improve the way for field teams document work, while automatically eliminating duplicate reporting and administrative work. (reduce from 14 required fields to 4)
-> Contribute to the OKR Reduce time spent creating weekly project status reports by 30%

The assumptions:
Simplifying the daily log from 14 required fields to 4 and removing duplicate data entry will reduce average completion time from 15 minutes to between 5 and 8 minutes per submission.
With at least 90% user adoption across 50 field workers, this is expected to generate 7-10 minutes of realized time savings per worker per day, equivalent to 1.0-1.5 FTEs of annual capacity.

The expected return:
Reducing the daily log from 14 required fields to 4 is expected to save approximately 10 minutes per field worker per day. Across 50 field workers, this equates to roughly 3,000 hours of productive capacity annually, or 1.5 FTEs of equivalent capacity.
Based on estimated labor rates, this represents $150,000-$300,000 in annual productivity value.

The initiative requires a one-time investment of $50,000 for design, development, testing, deployment, and training, plus an estimated $15,000 annually for adoption monitoring, process governance, user support, and continuous improvement activities to sustain usage and prevent reversion to duplicate reporting practices.

After accounting for the one-time investment and first-year retention effort, the expected net first-year benefit is $85,000-$235,000, with ongoing annual value of $135,000-$285,000 thereafter.

The expected payback period is approximately 2-4 months following full deployment.

Kill criterion

If, within 90 days of deployment to 50 field workers, measured productivity gains average less than 7 minutes per worker per day (equivalent to less than 1 FTE of capacity released annually), all new development stops and the remaining budget and engineering capacity are reassigned to higher-priority initiatives.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

Initially i thought the hardest part was not being concise enough, but actually it was making deliberate choices about what mattered most.
The course repeatedly challenged me to move from ideas/assumptions and feature requests to measurable outcomes and clear success criteria (including Hard No)
Defining OKRs, building a value-based business case, and establishing kill criteria exposed gaps in my thinking and decision-making.

Key learnings

The most valuable learning is the discipline of linking initiatives to strategy and measurable outcomes,
One additional key discipline, which i will have to 'force" myself in including is the validated (internal) customer value.
(Are we really solving for the (internal) customers?)

I will continue to use the frameworks to test assumptions, define expected outcomes before development starts, and evaluate whether investments are delivering the intended business impact. On top of this introduce the Kill Criterion
This will ensure the team, stakeholders as well as leadership are aware and onboard when a new initiative is stopped due to lack of value delivery.

LLMs can accelerate analysis and challenge thinking, but they are most valuable when combined with sound product judgment and customer insight. (Which was experienced a couple of times during the course.)

Aha moment

The initial reason for the chosen transformations to succeed or fail was not based on the right determinant,
The real determinant is whether my product becomes the easiest channel, not the required channel.

My biggest realization was the succes of a deployment (including adoption) is not sustained through mandates alone.
While requirements can drive initial usage, long-term success depends on whether the product delivers enough value that users naturally choose it over alternatives.
Ease of use is a critical component, but the broader determinant is whether the product becomes the most effective and valuable way for users to accomplish their goals.
(In fact i actually updated my actual product strategy accordingly ;-) )
